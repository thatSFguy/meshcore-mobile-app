Your saved settings

Kept in this browser only (local storage) — handy while you are experimenting. Nothing is sent anywhere, and clearing site data removes them.

    Transmit power is deliberately not included. Every radio field above is “match this or you are not on the mesh”. Power is not — it is the legal limit where the scanner is standing and what their hardware can do. Nor are channel keys: this is a config code, not a secret, so it is safe to print and post.

    These are settings a community has agreed on, not legal advice. Frequency and duty cycle are regulated where you are, and getting them wrong is an offence in most places.

    Apply over USB

    Plug a repeater, room server or companion node into this computer and write the settings straight to it — no QR, no radio link. Over USB the node takes commands without a login, because physical access already is the credential; it is also the safest place to retune a node, since a mistake is recoverable by unplugging it rather than by driving to a hilltop.

    Receiver sensitivity
    
    Sensitivity does not depend on frequency. It is set by
    bandwidth and spreading factor alone: thermal noise in the channel, plus the receiver's
    noise figure, plus how far below the noise that spreading factor can still decode.
    S = −174 + 10·log₁₀(BW) + NF + SNRmin, with NF taken
    as 6 dB. Frequency changes how much signal survives the journey, not what the far end can
    hear when it arrives — which is why there is no frequency column below.
    
    
    At 125 kHz this lands on the SX1262 datasheet's typical figures across
    SF7–SF12. SF5 and SF6 are extrapolated from the same 2.5 dB spacing and are the optimistic
    end of the table, not measured values.

    Reading it
    
    There is deliberately no distance here. Turning path loss
    into range needs terrain, antenna height and clutter; a free-space figure at these budgets
    comes out in the thousands of kilometres, which is worse than no answer. Use the decibels
    instead — 6 dB is twice the distance in free space and rather less than that in the real
    world.
    
    
    The path-loss figure is a ceiling with no fade margin: a link sitting
    exactly on it does not survive rain, a passing truck, or the leaves coming out. Real
    deployments plan 10–20 dB below it.
    
    
    Sensitivity on its own always says "use SF12", which is true and useless.
    Switch to the airtime view and the trade becomes visible: every 2.5 dB of reach costs
    roughly double the time on air, and the air is shared with everyone else on the mesh.
